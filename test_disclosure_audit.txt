TITLE: AUTONOMOUS ROBOTIC SURGICAL SYSTEM WITH REAL-TIME TISSUE COMPLIANCE AND DUAL-DRIVE ARTICULATION

FIELD OF THE INVENTION:
This invention relates to minimally invasive robotic surgery systems, specifically automated force-feedback control and tissue compliance estimation.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION:
Traditional surgical end effectors lack dynamic real-time telemetry to prevent tissue trauma during automated suturing and clamping. Existing instruments rely on manual surgeon tactile estimation, which is lost across laparoscopic teleoperation linkages.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION:
The present system comprises an articulated multi-axis surgical wrist, an array of piezoelectric impedance sensors embedded at the gripper jaws, an optical coherence tomography (OCT) subsurface scanner, and an adaptive FPGA-based closed-loop neural controller. The neural controller continuously modulates actuator displacement and clamping force at 1000 Hz based on real-time tissue elasticity gradients.

TECHNICAL CLAIMS:
1. An autonomous surgical instrument system comprising: an articulated multi-axis end effector; a piezoelectric sensor array positioned on the end effector configured to measure real-time tissue mechanical impedance; and a digital controller configured to dynamically modulate motor drive current based on said measured impedance.
2. The surgical instrument system of claim 1, further comprising an optical coherence tomography probe integrated coaxially within the end effector to generate subsurface depth profiles of target tissue.
3. The surgical instrument system of claim 1, wherein the digital controller executes an adaptive closed-loop impedance estimation algorithm at a control frequency of at least 1 kilohertz to prevent tissue shear.
